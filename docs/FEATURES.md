# mistLAB feature inventory

Round-zero QA assessment of the whole VTT as a DM and players use it at the table, measured against the built app (Playwright, software renderer) and against ten other virtual tabletops. Dates from the first pass of the five-version QA programme; see `docs/ROADMAP.md` for what each version changes.

## What mistLAB is

mistLAB is a browser/iPad-first 3D tabletop (Vite + TypeScript + three.js PWA) for a DM running Curse of Strahd (and a second small campaign, A Wild Sheep Chase) at a physical table: one screen the DM holds with a DM/Players slider and a "Players" mode to turn toward the table, plus an optional second-window Player Display synced over BroadcastChannel on the same device. It is a single-device, local-only tool (IndexedDB state, no server, no remote play): every Curse of Strahd map is modelled as original low-poly geometry organised chapter -> location -> area key with page references, a painted world map with travel times, fog/exploration, token movement with 5e rules, a creature repository, initiative with movement/reach overlays, and a calendar/clock with day-night.

## Strengths

- Complete coverage of the module's geography: all 39 Curse of Strahd locations plus Death House build and open without errors, organised exactly like the book (chapter, location, area key, page number on every room), so the DM can find 'K41' or 'ritual' in two keystrokes.
- Strong DM-vs-players separation at the content level: slider and Players mode hide labels, notes, hidden objects and unrevealed creatures; info cards carry player-facing wording and DM-only notes separately; the Player Display window is pinned to the players' view and follows the DM's camera, level and location.
- Table-ready rules support: 5e vision/light model with torch and darkvision, grid snapping, measure tool, initiative with movement budgets and reach/range overlays, locked doors and walls that actually block the players' token, creature repository with AC/HP/CR and hidden-until-revealed placement.
- Section slicer and stacked floors make multi-storey buildings legible (inn, mansion, windmill, castle), with detents per floor, Whole building, and furniture never sliced.
- Campaign clock is genuinely useful at the table: Harptos calendar, moon phase, day/night lighting on outdoor maps, and a travel calculator that advances time by the PHB pace table.
- Offline-capable PWA with all scene data bundled; state (reveals, doors, containers, tokens, roster, clock, world position) persists across reloads and per campaign; a second campaign switches cleanly.
- Painted world map with distances, pace-based travel times, trail of moves, DM-controlled reveal of places, and one-tap opening of the matching battle map.

## Weaknesses

- Navigation into buildings from town maps is effectively broken by tap (only 3 of 13 Vallaki sites reachable), and multi-floor stairs skip floors, both of which a DM hits in the first session.
- Phone layout is unusable for the camera/help buttons and dropdown menus; the product is iPad-first and the 390 px width shows overlaps everywhere, so a DM on a phone or a narrow split-screen iPad will struggle.
- Players mode leaks: floor dropdown, section slider, Library and World map stay available to players; the players' turn bar names hidden creatures; unexplored walls show the dungeon's silhouette; the world map's painted symbols reveal unrevealed settlements.
- No remote play: everything is one device/browser (BroadcastChannel, IndexedDB); remote players cannot see or move anything.
- Rules reference is thin: only AC/HP/speed/CR for open-content monsters, no stat blocks, no spell/condition tooltips, no dice, no page-linked text (the DM still needs the book open), and the diagonal rule has no setting.
- Legibility at human scale needs work: label collisions on the world map and between token tags and room keys, DM notes clipped under the top bar, Castle Ravenloft rendered as a grey slab (scene flagged partial), crude mist-wall visuals on the Old Svalich Road.
- Hex grid is second-class: no range overlay, stale square overlay when switched mid-encounter, hex snapping unverified.
- Precision tapping is required for small things (secret doors behind furniture, chests against walls, hidden creature slots) with no list-based fallback other than rooms.

### The other tabletops, briefly

- **Foundry VTT**: Foundry VTT is a self-hosted (or partner-hosted), browser-played 2D virtual tabletop sold as a one-time $50 GM license; players join free in a desktop browser. Current stable is Version 14 (April 2026); v13 (2025) brought a UI overhaul, Scene Regions and drag-to-measure token movement, and v14 added native multi-level "Scene Levels", Scene Regions v2 and pop-out windows. Its core strengths are a best-in-class walls/lighting/vision/fog engine, a huge module ecosystem (350+ game systems, thousands of add-ons) built on a documented JavaScript API with Documents, flags and Hooks, and a growing catalogue of first-party premium content. Since Feb 2024 Wizards of the Coast publishes official D&D 5e products on Foundry (Phandelver and Below, Tomb of Annihilation, 2024 PHB/DMG/MM, Heroes/Adventures in Faerun, Eberron, Ravenloft: The Horrors Within), typically at $29.99 each; Paizo sells PF2e Adventure Paths (Abomination Vaults $54.99, or $21 if you own the PDF). As of Oct 2026 there is NO official Curse of Strahd module; CoS on Foundry means community content (free Death House modules from Eidolon/DragnaCarta and The MAD Cartographer, Dungeons LAB map packs) or importing your own D&D Beyond purchase via the DDB-Importer module. Official adventures are delivered as pre-walled, pre-lit Scenes with high-res maps and weather, 150+ dynamic-ring tokens, and the adventure text converted into interlinked, tooltipped Journal Entries pinned to the map as Map Notes; the ToA module lets the DM pick 2014 or 2024 rules at initialization. Users praise the lighting engine, performance, one-time price, system breadth and module freedom; they complain about setup/learning curve, hosting hassle, no native iPad/mobile support, modules breaking on major updates, resource use on weak hardware, and thin built-in accessibility. - **Roll20**: Roll20 (launched 2012) is the most widely used browser-based virtual tabletop: free to start, no install, runs on a hosted server with a freemium subscription (Plus/Pro) gating dynamic lighting, advanced fog, Reactions, storage and the Mod (API) scripting sandbox. In 2025 it rebuilt its rendering engine ("Jumpgate", now simply the default tabletop for all new games), added a Foreground Layer (rooftops, canopies, weather overlays that fade when a token moves under them), Reactions (token-overlap triggers that fire text, rolls or macros), Map Pins (handout sections pinned to map locations with a GM-notes/player-text split), Dungeon Scrawl integration, a Discord Activity, and a new D&D 5e/2024 sheet plus the acquired Demiplane character/compendium platform. It remains a strictly 2D top-down VTT: no 3D, no native elevation or multi-level play (explicitly still "being worked on"), no offline mode, and a dedicated mobile app was retired in favor of browser/tablet play. Its biggest commercial strength is the marketplace of licensed, pre-built WotC adventures; Curse of Strahd ($24.99, remastered 2025) is a flagship example with 47 dynamic-lit maps, map pins for every keyed area, cross-linked chapter handouts, pre-placed NPC tokens with sheets, a virtual Tarokka deck, rollable tables and automated traps, plus a readable digital copy on D&D Nexus. Users praise its zero-friction onboarding, free tier, huge community and content library; they complain about performance on large maps with dynamic lighting, a dated UI, subscription creep/price increases, lighting bugs, and weak accessibility. Standout: ['Zero-install, free-to-start, runs in any browser; the easiest VTT for beginners and the largest player base/LFG community', 'Massive library of licensed, fully pre-built official adventures (WotC, Paizo, Chaosium etc.) that are ready to run on purchase', 'Cross-linked handouts and Map Pins make prepared modules fast to run at the table', 'Hosted sync with integrated voice/video or Discord Activity; players need nothing but a link', 'Deep macro/dice language, roll templates and (on Pro) the Mod API script library with huge community automation', 'Active 2025 development cadence: new engine, Foreground Layer, Reactions, Map Pins, Dungeon Scrawl, D&D 2024 sheet and Demiplane integration', 'Complaints: performance degradation and lag on large maps with dynamic lighting or many tokens; dated/cluttered UI; dynamic lighting bugs (overexposure, darkvision); subscription required for core GM features and repeated price increases; API locked to Pro; no native elevation/multi-level or 3D; no offline mode; weak accessibility; mobile app retired; blurry maps and measurement quirks on the new engine'] Pricing: Free tier (100 MB storage, basic fog of war, map pins, unlimited games/players). Weaknesses: a dense, engineer-designed UI with a steep learning curve, no tablet/mobile/browser play (only a browser-based Online Reader for purchased books), heavy RAM/load-time footprint, slow homebrew creation, and lower visual polish than Foundry. - **Owlbear Rodeo (2.x, web-based; current release 2.4)**: Owlbear Rodeo is a deliberately minimal, system-agnostic, browser-based "battle map first" VTT built by a two-person team. Its philosophy is to get out of the way: a GM uploads a map, drops tokens, draws fog and is running an encounter in minutes, with no installs and no player accounts required. Core features are rooms (the persistent space players join) and scenes (an infinite canvas with a map, grid, tokens, drawings, fog, rulers and notes), a layered image system (Map, Prop, Mount, Character, Attachment, Note), a flexible grid (square, hex vertical/horizontal, isometric, dimetric) with per-scene measurement rules, real-time synced drawing and rulers, a built-in dice roller, static fog, and, since 2.3's GPU "Warp Core" renderer, native dynamic fog primitives (walls, doors, lights, token-based vision, soft shadows, collision). 2.4 added "Forecast" computer-vision automatic fog detection and a Storage Saver (WebP conversion). Almost everything beyond the map is delegated to a 119-item extension ecosystem (initiative, HP/conditions, 3D dice, character sheets, D&D Beyond bridges, audio, weather, measuring tools, journals, DM screens) built on a TypeScript SDK. It has no rules engine, no built-in character sheets or compendium, no official adventure modules (Curse of Strahd is not sold; DMs load their own maps), no built-in voice/video, and no offline mode in 2.x (the open-source Legacy 1.0 stored assets in IndexedDB and can be self-hosted). Pricing is a free tier plus two cheap storage/room tiers. It is widely recommended as the easiest VTT for beginners, one-shots and in-person "cast to TV" play, and criticized for lacking persistent campaign tooling, sheets, automation and for the move from local storage to a subscription cloud model.
